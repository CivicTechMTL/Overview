# Contributing to Civic Tech Montréal

Thanks for your interest! Civic Tech Montréal is a volunteer community, and
there's room for everyone: coders, designers, researchers, policy folks,
community organizers, and anyone who cares about how Montréal works.
**You don't need to know how to code to contribute.**

## Ways to contribute

**Show up.** Come to a meetup. Check [Luma](https://luma.com/civictechmontreal)
for the next event. Introduce yourself and tell us what interests you.

**Share a problem.** Know a civic issue in your neighbourhood, organization or
workplace? We're looking for problem partners, not just builders.
[Pitch a civic problem](../../issues/new/choose) in this repo, post in Slack, or bring it to a meetup.

**Join a project.** Browse our [projects on GitHub](https://github.com/CivicTechMTL).
Each project's README explains what it's about and how to help. Not sure where
to start? Ask in [Slack](https://join.slack.com/t/civictechmontreal/shared_invite/zt-3sx2os89v-6rIwr2sNmhl6r2FZvyPoCQ)
or at a meetup and we'll connect you with the people involved.

**Help run the community.** Greeting newcomers, hosting meetups, finding
venues or speakers — see the [volunteer roles](https://github.com/CivicTechMTL/Organizing/tree/main/Roles).

**Improve our docs.** Spotted something outdated, confusing or missing?
Suggest a change (see below). Fixing a typo counts!

## Contributing on GitHub

> **New to GitHub?** Start with our [GitHub 101](GITHUB-101.md): what GitHub
> is, the key terms, and a step-by-step guide to your first contribution.

1. **Start with an issue.** Before making a big change, [open an issue](../../issues/new/choose)
   to describe your idea or question. This lets others weigh in and avoids
   duplicate work. Small fixes (typos, broken links) can skip this step.
2. **Make your change on a branch, not on `main`.** If you don't have write
   access, GitHub will create a copy (a *fork*) for you automatically when
   you click the ✏️ edit button on any file.
3. **Open a pull request.** Describe what you changed and why, and link the
   issue if there is one.
4. **Ask for a review.** Someone from the team will look at it, may suggest
   edits, and will merge it when it's ready.

Each project may have its own setup or extra guidelines — check its README first.

## How we work together

- **Be kind and patient.** Everyone is learning, including the organizers.
- **Ask questions.** There's no such thing as a silly question here.
- **Work in the open.** Share progress and decisions where others can see them
  (issues, Slack, meeting notes) so people can step in and out easily.
- **Credit others.** Acknowledge ideas and work that aren't yours.
- **English or French.** Contribute in whichever language you're comfortable with.

All contributors are expected to follow our [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
